Iconic Species of Sri Lanka

Discover the magnificent wildlife that makes Sri Lanka one of Asia's premier safari destinations, with some of the highest densities of key species in the world.

Sri Lankan Leopard

Yala & Wilpattu National Parks

With one of the highest leopard densities in the world, Sri Lanka offers unparalleled opportunities to observe these majestic predators in their natural habitat.

Asian Elephant

Minneriya, Udawalawe & Kaudulla

Witness the spectacular "Gathering" where hundreds of elephants congregate, or observe family herds in protected parks throughout the island.

Blue Whale

Mirissa & Trincomalee Coast

Experience the awe of encountering the largest animal on Earth in the deep waters off Sri Lanka's coast, one of the world's best whale watching destinations.

Premier National Parks

From dense dry zone forests to coastal wilderness, each park offers unique ecosystems and wildlife viewing opportunities.

Yala National Park

Best for: Leopard sightings

  • Highest leopard density in the world
  • Diverse ecosystems from jungle to beach
  • Sloth bears, crocodiles, and over 200 bird species
  • Exclusive private safari routes available

Udawalawe National Park

Best for: Elephant encounters

  • Guaranteed elephant sightings year-round
  • Udawalawe Elephant Transit Home visit
  • Water birds and buffalo herds
  • Excellent for photography

Wilpattu National Park

Best for: Wilderness experience

  • Sri Lanka's largest national park
  • Natural lakes (villus) attract wildlife
  • Less crowded, more authentic experience
  • Leopards, bears, and spotted deer

Safari Planning Questions

What is the best time for wildlife viewing in Sri Lanka?
The dry seasons (January-April in the south/east, May-September in the north) offer the best wildlife viewing as animals congregate around water sources. However, different parks have optimal viewing at different times. Yala is best January-July, while Wilpattu peaks February-October. Whale watching is best November-April in Mirissa and March-September in Trincomalee.
Are your safaris suitable for photographers?
Absolutely. We offer photography-focused safaris with guides who understand lighting, positioning, and animal behavior for optimal shots. Vehicles are equipped with camera mounts, and we schedule drives during golden hours. Private vehicles ensure you have space for equipment and can stay longer at sightings.
What should we bring on safari?
Neutral-colored clothing (khaki, green, brown), binoculars, camera with zoom lens (300mm+ recommended), sunscreen, hat, and light jacket for early mornings. We provide water and snacks. For bird watchers, we recommend a field guide and notebook.
Can you accommodate families with children?
Yes, we design family-friendly safaris with age-appropriate activities. We can arrange shorter drives, educational activities at lodges, and visits to ethical wildlife rehabilitation centers. Minimum age requirements vary by park (typically 6+ for regular safaris).
How do you ensure ethical wildlife viewing?
We follow strict guidelines: maintain safe distances, limit vehicle numbers at sightings, avoid feeding or disturbing animals, use lodges that follow sustainable practices, and employ local guides who respect wildlife. We're certified by the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority for ethical wildlife tourism.
